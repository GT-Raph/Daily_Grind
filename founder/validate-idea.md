# Validate idea: websites for independent cafés in Accra

Assumption: the product is a website built for each café (menu, hours, location, reviews, contact), sold by you as a one-time build or a monthly plan.
Assumption: you have no paying customers yet. The Daily Grind site is a demo.

## The 30-second assessment

This is a real service, but a weak startup. Cafés in Accra get found on Google Maps, Instagram and WhatsApp, not on websites. The Daily Grind has a 4.9 rating from 42 reviews and no website at all: its listing still says "Add website". The pain is small, and there are only about 130 customers to sell to.

## The five fatal questions

**Q1: Who is the customer, and would they pay for this today?**

The customer is the owner and operator of a specialty café with one location in East Legon, Osu, Airport or Spintex. They are usually opened in the last three years, with a menu that changes and a crowd that looks them up online.

Some would pay, because Ghanaian agencies already charge for this. Small business sites cost GH₵2,000 to GH₵4,500, and many SMEs spend GH₵3,000 to GH₵7,000 with hosting ([Elysia Ghana, 2026](https://elysiaghana.com/website-design-cost-ghana/), [Faciotech, 2026](https://blog.faciotech.com/website-cost-in-ghana)). But it's a one-time purchase, and most owners will say "maybe, later", because Instagram is free and already works. Willingness to pay a monthly fee: unproven, so treat it as a no.

**Q2: Why hasn't someone built this already?**

People have, many times:
- [Popmenu](https://orderitto.com/compare/popmenu-pricing) sells restaurant websites at US$179 to US$499 a month in the US.
- [BentoBox](https://restauranttools.ai/tools/bentobox) did the same and is now only sold to Clover POS customers.
- Google gave every business a free website and shut down over 21 million of them in 2024, citing low engagement ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-shutting-down-google-business-profiles-websites-36686.html)). That is the strongest warning here: free and built in still wasn't used.
- Locally, dozens of agencies build sites for SMEs ([Think Expand](https://www.expandgh.com/website-design-prices-in-ghana/), [GhanaWeb](https://www.ghanaweb.com/GhanaHomePage/features/Cost-of-web-design-in-Ghana-and-why-most-SMEs-don-t-need-a-complex-website-2005113)).
- Platforms struggle in Ghana too: Glovo left in 2025, and Bolt Food remains ([TechLabari](https://techlabari.com/glovo-gears-up-to-capture-the-food-delivery-space-due-to-dwindling-competition-in-ghana/)).

What's different now: specialty cafés are growing. Ghana's coffee shop count rose 19.4% from 2023 to 158 by April 2026 ([Rentech Digital](https://rentechdigital.com/smartscraper/business-report-details/list-of-coffee-shops-in-ghana)). That's real, but it's growth on a tiny base.

**Q3: What's the distribution advantage?**

This is the best part. Cafés are physical, close together, and listed on Google Maps with a visible "no website" gap. You can walk into 20 in a week with a demo on your phone. Owners know each other, so referrals will happen.

There's no growth loop, though. Each sale is a visit, a pitch and a build. A small "Site by [you]" footer link could bring some leads, but expect manual selling forever.

**Q4: Can this be a big business, or is it a feature?**

It's a feature. Instagram, Google Business Profile and Bolt Food already cover menus, hours, photos and ordering. A website builder like Wix gives the rest away.

The ceiling:
- Estimate: 127 coffee shops in Greater Accra ([Rentech Digital](https://rentechdigital.com/smartscraper/business-report-details/list-of-coffee-shops-in-ghana)) × 30% who buy × GH₵3,000 = about GH₵114,000 in total, once.
- Estimate: even if every one paid GH₵300 a month, that's 127 × 300 × 12 = GH₵457,200 a year.
- $1M ARR is roughly GH₵12M a year. Estimate: this assumes about GH₵12 per US$1, so check the current rate. That would need about 3,300 businesses paying GH₵300 a month. Accra cafés alone can't get there. You'd need all food businesses across West Africa, and a product that does far more than a website.

**Q5: Can the founder actually build this?**

The build is the easy part: we made a working demo in a day. The hard parts are operational:
- getting real menus, prices and photos out of busy owners
- keeping sites up to date when menus change, which is what makes a monthly fee worth paying
- collecting payment, probably by mobile money

There's no first-hire problem. It's a sales and service problem.

## Validation experiments

**1. Will owners pay before you build?**
- Test: café owners will put down money for a site based on a demo.
- How: list Accra cafés on Google Maps with no website. Visit 20 of them in person with the Daily Grind demo on your phone, and get the owner's permission before you show it as your work. Offer a site in 7 days for GH₵2,500, with GH₵500 paid upfront by mobile money.
- Success: 3 deposits from 20 visits.
- Time and cost: 1 week, about GH₵600 for transport.

**2. One-off or monthly?**
- Test: owners will pay monthly for upkeep.
- How: in the same visits, offer two options side by side: GH₵2,500 once, or GH₵250 a month including unlimited menu and hours updates sent by WhatsApp.
- Success: at least 40% of interested owners choose monthly.
- Time and cost: same visits, GH₵0.

**3. Does a site bring customers?**
- Test: a website actually drives orders or visits.
- How: with The Daily Grind owner's agreement, deploy the demo free on Vercel or Netlify and add it to the Google listing. Use a WhatsApp order button with a tracked link, and count clicks on directions and calls.
- Success: 20 or more order or call clicks a week by week two.
- Time and cost: 2 weeks, under GH₵100 for a domain.

If experiment 3 fails, the website isn't the product. Ordering is.

## The honest verdict

**Pivot it.** As a startup, "websites for Accra cafés" has a ceiling under GH₵500,000 a year. As a side business it's fine: good money per job, and easy to find customers.

The stronger angle is to sell **orders, not websites**. For example, a WhatsApp and mobile money ordering link for every independent food business in Accra, including restaurants, bakeries and chop bars, not just the 127 cafés. It would include a simple menu page and charge a small monthly fee or a per-order fee. That gives a reason to pay every month, a much larger market, and a growth loop, because every customer who orders sees the link.

This week: run experiment 1 with 20 cafés. In every visit, ask one question: "How do customers order from you today, and what goes wrong?"
